Generating text with a frozen embedding model

A frozen EmbeddingGemma 2 encoder reads the conversation and turns it into per-token states, the map. A small trained decoder, the navigator, reads that map and writes the reply, 16 tokens per encoder pass, choosing each token by pointing into the encoder's own token-embedding table. The encoder receives no gradients. Below, every sampled token is shown with its probability and the points where the encoder re-reads the context.

    Method

    A trained navigator on a frozen map

    The encoder is bidirectional, so it cannot generate left to right by itself. Instead it is run once per chunk: it reads the entire conversation and returns per-token hidden states (the "map"). The navigator, a 4-layer causal transformer, then takes up to 16 steps, self-attending to the tokens it has written in the chunk and cross-attending to the map. Each step scores candidate tokens by a dot product with the encoder's frozen embedding table. After 16 tokens the encoder re-reads the context, now including the new text.

    Training used two stages: about 200k social dialogues (SODA, DailyDialog) for fluency, then 28k assistant-style and empathetic conversations, with loss on assistant turns only.

    Limitations

    What to expect

    • Fluent, shallow. Small talk, follow-up questions and supportive replies work; factual questions ("why is the sky blue?") get on-topic but meaningless answers.
    • Short memory. It can lose the thread after two or three turns; the context window is 384 tokens.
    • Sampling matters. Temperature around 0.5 gives the most coherent replies; higher values expose low-probability tokens, visible as short bars in the trace.
    • Scale. About 12M training tokens on one laptop GPU. A capable assistant would need a far larger navigator and orders of magnitude more data.
    Encoder: google/embeddinggemma-2 (Apache-2.0). Navigator trained on SODA, DailyDialog, everyday-conversations, Empathetic Dialogues. Research demo, not for production use.
